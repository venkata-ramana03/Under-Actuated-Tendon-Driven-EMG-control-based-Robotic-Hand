# Under-Actuated-Tendon-Driven-EMG-control-based-Robotic-Hand
Designed and prototyped a low-cost, 3D-printable, modular 5-DOF underactuated tendon-driven prosthetic hand. Engineered for easy manufacturing and repair in resource-constrained settings, targeting a 23-grasp-type capability.

Designed and developed an under-actuated, tendon-driven robotic hand with a minimum five-degree-of-freedom (5-DOF) architecture for performing essential grasping tasks. The proposed five-DOF architecture was capable of achieving 23 different grasp configurations.

As the first contributor during the early phase of the project, I was involved in the initial project planning, mechanical architecture development, DOF configuration, and CAD design of the robotic hand. The design focused on achieving a higher range of grasping capabilities with a reduced number of actuators through under-actuated tendon-driven mechanisms.

The system involved EMG-based control and integration of multiple mechatronic components, including servo motors, DC motors, motor drivers, microcontrollers, and associated electronic hardware.
